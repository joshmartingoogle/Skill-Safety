# Skill-Safety: Assisted vs. Unassisted Skills Empirical Pipeline & Results Brief

This repository contains the directionally standardized **Skill Safety Index Rubric**, the Python scoring and psychometric evaluation pipeline (`pipeline.py` and `analyze_and_plot.py`), the latest occupational task and skill taxonomies (**Stanford TaskNet**, **O\*NET 31.0 Tasks**, and **O\*NET 31.0 Skills**), all statistical and visual artefacts (with all decimals rounded to one decimal place), and the 4-page LaTeX results brief (`skill_safety_results_brief.tex` / `skill_safety_results_brief.pdf`).

## Key Findings Upfront (All Decimals Rounded to One Decimal Place)

- **Directional Standardization & Certainty-Weighted Scoring ($N=530$ Items):** All six scored dimensions of the Skill Safety Index (`N1` Fallback, `N2` Live Performance, `N3` No-AI Rules, `N4` Checking, `N5` Foundation, and `S` Stakes) are oriented consistently on a `0`–`3` scale where **higher scores indicate greater skill safety** (lower unassisted need and lower error stakes: `3` = Safest / Assist for Good, `0` = Least Safe / Must Retain Unassisted Ability). Passing every item individually to the Gemini Flash API yields high epistemic certainty ($\bar{c} = 0.9$).
- **Bimodal Overall Distributions Across Taxonomies:** Certainty-weighted Skill Safety Index scores ($I_i \in [0,3]$) exhibit a bimodal distribution across **Stanford TaskNet** ($\text{Mean}=1.2$, $\text{SD}=0.9$, $\text{Median}=1.5$, $\text{IQR}=1.8$), **O\*NET 31.0 Tasks** ($\text{Mean}=1.2$, $\text{SD}=0.9$, $\text{Median}=1.2$, $\text{IQR}=1.5$), and **O\*NET 31.0 Skills** ($\text{Mean}=1.3$, $\text{SD}=1.1$, $\text{Median}=1.2$, $\text{IQR}=2.0$). Across taxonomies, $51.0\%\text{--}54.8\%$ of items fall in the low-safety regime ($I_i < 1.5$), while $21.2\%\text{--}34.3\%$ sit in the high-safety regime ($I_i \ge 2.0$).
- **Pipeline Forensics — Why Is the Distribution Bimodal?:** Multi-stage diagnostic decomposition (`output/bimodality_diagnostic_stages.csv`) demonstrates that **roughly two-thirds of the apparent bimodality is an artifact of three data pipeline and cleaning choices**, while the remaining one-third reflects genuine within-occupation task structure:
  1. *Artifact 1 — Forced Scoring of Non-AI-Assistable (`Gate = False`) Physical/Observational Items ($19.8\%$ of pool, $105/530$):* Forcing `0`–`3` rubric scores on physical tasks that fail the AI-assistability gate (e.g., Dishwashers scraping plates, Care Aides bathing patients) causes the LLM to assign `0` across live/fallback/checking dimensions (`65.7%` score $< 0.5$ and `35.2%` score `0.0`, accounting for `43.0%` of all exact zeros). Applying the rubric's Gate filter removes this artificial lower spike.
  2. *Artifact 2 — O\*NET 31.0 `Scale ID = 'IM'` Missing `Not Relevant` Flags ($31.9\%$ of O\*NET Skills, $67/210$):* In O\*NET 31.0, `Not Relevant == 'Y'` is populated only on `LV` (Level) rows and is blank on `IM` (Importance) rows. Selecting `IM` rows formed a full $35 \times 6 = 210$ Cartesian product that retained $67$ irrelevant occupation-skill pairs (e.g., *Programming* for Dishwashers, *Repairing* for Lawyers). Because irrelevant skills are never performed live and carry no stakes in those jobs, the LLM scored them at $\text{Mean}=2.1$ ($65.7\%$ scoring $\ge 2.2$) vs. $\text{Mean}=1.0$ for relevant skills, creating an artificial upper mode near $2.8\text{--}3.0$.
  3. *Artifact 3 — Single-Call Context Halo in Emergency Medicine (`29-1214.00`) ($79.6\%$ of Gated Zeros, $39/49$):* Scoring all six dimensions in a single prompt call per item induces a zero-floor halo in acute clinical medicine (`52.7%` of gated EM items receive `[0,0,0,0,0,0]`, including `N3=0` via generalized HIPAA citations). Removing this halo alongside Gate and relevance cleaning reduces exact `0.0` scores from $13.8\%\text{--}18.6\%$ to **$2.6\%\text{--}3.1\%$** and lowers Sarle's Bimodality Coefficient ($\text{BC}$) below the $0.6$ bimodality threshold in O\*NET Tasks ($\text{BC}=0.5$, unimodal).
  4. *Genuine Structural Driver — Synchronous Delivery vs. Asynchronous Prep:* Within individual non-EM desk occupations, **Software Developers** ($\text{BC}=0.4$, $\text{Mean}=1.9$) and **Lawyers** ($\text{BC}=0.4$, $\text{Mean}=1.4$) are unimodal, whereas **Teachers** ($\text{BC}=0.7$) and **Personal Care Aides** ($\text{BC}=0.6$) remain genuinely bimodal due to the split between live interpersonal care/instruction ($I_i \approx 0.3$) and asynchronous planning/documentation ($I_i \approx 2.0$).
- **Scale Reliability (Cronbach's $\alpha$) & Leave-One-Out Diagnostics:** Cronbach's $\alpha_{\text{CW}}$ is **$0.9$** for Stanford TaskNet, **$0.9$** for O\*NET 31.0 Tasks, and **$0.9$** for O\*NET 31.0 Skills (all $> 0.7$). Leave-one-out diagnostics show that **Stakes ($S$)** and **Foundation ($N_5$)** have the lowest item-rest correlations ($r_{\text{rest}} = 0.5\text{--}0.6$).
- **Spot Verification Audit ($n=45$ Stratified Subsample):** Independent second-pass verification across $45$ randomly sampled items ($15$ per taxonomy) achieves **$100.0\%$ gate agreement**, **$95.9\%$ within-$\pm 1$ dimension agreement** ($83.3\%$ exact), and index Pearson $r = 0.9$ ($\text{MAE} = 0.2$).
